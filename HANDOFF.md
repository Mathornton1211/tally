# Tally

A self-hosted Mint replacement. Pulls every account through Plaid, runs all AI
locally, and spends most of its effort on the things Mint never did well:
finding fees, stopping them, spotting fraud, and saying which accounts to
close.

This document is the spec and the source of truth. If it conflicts with a
comment in the code, this wins.

---

**Funds: saving for a specific thing (2026-09-17).** Migration 012, `tally/funds.py`, `/funds`.
- A fund is one purchase (3D printer, truck tires) with a target and an optional date. **Budgets (2026-09-17).** Migration 013, `tally/budgets.py`, `/budget`. The gap that made Tally not-a-Mint-replacement.
- A budget row is the amount that starts applying in that month and keeps applying until another row replaces it. One row per change, not 12 a year, and changing next month never rewrites how last month was judged.
- Rollover is per category. Off = Mint (fresh every month). On = envelope (leftover AND overspend carry). Car repairs, medical and gifts only work with it on.
- **The bug worth remembering:** a naive run-rate projection (`spent/elapsed*days`) said $2,150 of rent on day 17 was "heading for $3,794" and raised 9 pace alerts out of 14 categories. Rent went out on the 1st and is not going out again. Fix: subtract the recurring charges that already landed, extrapolate only the day-to-day remainder, and add back what is still committed. 9 alerts became 2, both real.
- **Second bug in the same area:** monthly cadence is 30.4 days, and `round()` makes that 30, which fits twice inside a 31-day month -- so rent got committed twice. `_occurrences` now steps monthly/quarterly/yearly by calendar month and anchors on `last_date` (an observed date) rather than `next_date` (a prediction).
- `committed` (recurring not yet landed) is subtracted from what is left. No mainstream budget app does this, and it is the difference between "$135 left" and "$135 left but $120 of bills are coming".
- Suggestions: median of the last 3 *complete* months, rounded to something sayable ($410, not $412.37). An empty budget page is abandoned in week one.
- Tests: `tests/test_budgets.py` (21).

**A front door (2026-09-17).** `tally/auth.py`, `README.md`, `docker-compose.yml`, `scripts/install.sh`.
- Tally had no auth of its own -- correct on the home server (Authentik in front), useless anywhere else. `TALLY_AUTH=proxy` (default, unchanged for the homelab) or `password`.
- The session is a Fernet token in an HttpOnly cookie. Fernet already signs and timestamps, so the TTL is the expiry: no session table, no new dependency, no hand-rolled crypto. The password is scrypt'd with the install's Fernet key as salt.
- `docker-compose.yml` (root) is the standalone stack: no external network, no Traefik, named volume, binds to 127.0.0.1. `deploy/compose.yaml` remains the homelab one. Do not merge them.
- `scripts/install.sh` generates the secrets, prompts for a password and the Plaid keys, builds, waits for `/healthz`.
- Tests: `tests/test_auth.py` (10).

**Households (2026-09-17).** Migrations 014 + 018, `tally/people.py`, `tally/scope.py`, `/household`.
- The model is small on purpose: an account belongs to the household (`owner_id IS NULL`) or to one person. Transactions, balances and every chart inherit it. No per-transaction ownership — nobody tags groceries.
- **The privacy rule: an account with an owner is visible to that person alone, including not to a household `owner` role.** The role administers people and connections; it does not read anyone's bank account. A private option with an admin backdoor is not one, and the two senses of "owner" colliding in English is exactly how that gets implemented wrong.
- Enforcement is one predicate, `tally_can_see(owner_id)`, read by `v_txn` and `v_acct`. Route modules take their connection from `scope.connection()`, which sets `tally.viewer` transaction-locally from a ContextVar the auth middleware fills. No route signature mentions the viewer and no query has to remember to filter. The pool must NOT be autocommit — a transaction-local setting outside a transaction silently does nothing, and "silently does nothing" here means "shows everyone everything".
- **The bug this round was always going to have:** migration 014 filtered `v_txn`, which covers every transaction and total — and missed the dozen places reading the `accounts` table directly for balances. Net worth, the debt list, fees, investments and the assistant would each have shown a partner's private *balance* while correctly hiding every transaction in it. Migration 018 adds `v_acct` and every read path points at it. The fix was not to remember the filter twenty times; it was to make the filtered read the easy one with the obvious name.
- Auth is now per-person (`tally/auth.py`): each person has a login, and the session says who is asking. `TALLY_PASSWORD` still bootstraps the first person on an empty table, so an existing single-password install becomes a household of one with nobody doing anything.
- In `TALLY_AUTH=proxy` (the home server) the person switcher is a **view, not a wall** — anyone past Authentik can view as anyone. Right trade for a single-admin homelab, wrong one for a shared household. Documented in auth.py and on the settings page.
- Tests: `tests/test_household.py` (11), `tests/test_auth.py` (13).

**Multi-currency (2026-09-17).** Migration 015, `tally/money.py`.
- A transaction is stored in the currency it happened in, forever. `v_txn.amount` is the converted number every total adds up; `original_amount` keeps what the bank showed. A rate typed in today must not rewrite what last year's holiday cost, so rates are dated and the one in force is the most recent at or before the transaction date.
- Rates are entered, not fetched. Tally does not phone out, and a self-hosted finance app that silently depended on a third-party rate endpoint would trade away the property it is built on.
- A currency with no rate converts at 1 and is **flagged**, not silently multiplied — the settings page names exactly which ones are wrong.
- `tally_home_currency()` and `tally_rate()` are SECURITY DEFINER: `v_txn` is also read by the `tally_reader` role the assistant queries under, and that role deliberately cannot read tables.

**Rules (2026-09-17).** Migration 016, `tally/rules.py`, `/rules`.
- Renaming, categorising, tagging and noting are computed in `v_txn`, so a rule applies to history the instant it is saved and un-applies the instant it is deleted. No backfill job, nothing to get out of step.
- Splitting cannot work that way — a split is real child rows and a view cannot invent rows — so splits are applied after a sync by the worker. A split somebody edited by hand (`split_locked`) is never touched: having the computer undo your correction every six hours is worse than not automating it.
- A malformed regex matches nothing rather than breaking every query that reads a transaction.
- `category_rules` (005) is kept alongside: it is what the drawer's "apply to all from this merchant" writes, it is exact-match and index-friendly, and one engine serving both ends up worse at both.
- Precedence, hardest-won: a name or category the person typed themselves still beats a rule.

**Goals with a trajectory (2026-09-17).** Migration 017, `tally/goals.py`, `/goals`.
- Extends the existing `goals` table from 006 rather than adding a rival — two tables both called goals is how an app gets two answers to one question.
- The pace is **measured**, from how net worth actually moved, so a goal gets less optimistic on its own after a bad month. Deliberately first-to-last over the window rather than a least-squares fit: the series is walked back from today's balances, so its early values carry the most accumulated error and a regression would weight that error as signal.
- A flat trajectory yields no ETA rather than a date in the year 4000.
- `debt_free` counts DOWN — `remaining` is the debt itself and progress is the number shrinking. An emergency fund target in months of spending resolves to a number now, so it tracks life instead of going stale when rent rises.

**Sinking funds (2026-09-17).** Migration 017, `funds.auto_fill()`.
- Per paycheck, a percentage of income, or monthly. `auto_through` makes it idempotent — the worker runs it after every sync, and a fund that doubles every six hours is worse than doing it by hand. It stops at the target rather than overshooting, and a bought fund stops taking.
- The preview runs the instruction against real past income, because "10% of income" is an abstraction until you see what it would actually have set aside.

**Share links (2026-09-17).** Migration 017, `tally/share.py`.
- For the accountant who needs last year's numbers and not a login. A bearer token, treated like one: one date range, read-only, revocable, expiring by default, stored **hashed** so a database dump does not hand over working links, and view-counted so an unexpected open is visible.
- `as_person` freezes what the link may see to what its creator could see when they made it. Linking a private account next month must not silently widen a link already sitting in somebody else's inbox.
- `/shared/:token` renders outside the app layout — no sidebar, no nav, no way into the rest of the app.

**Search in a sentence (2026-09-17).** `tally/search.py`, `/search`.
- Two layers, and the order matters. Dates, amounts, categories, tags and quoted phrases are parsed from the text with no model at all — so search works with `AI_ENABLED=0`, answers instantly, and gives the model less to get wrong. The model is asked only for what text parsing could not get, and only ever to produce *filters*.
- **Totals are computed in SQL from the matched rows**, so the number on screen can never be a hallucination. The filters it used are always returned and shown as chips, so a wrong reading is visible and correctable rather than a silently wrong answer.
- A model that is down, slow or confused falls back to the text-parsed filters instead of failing.

**Roles, enforced once (2026-09-17).** `auth.permission()`, called by the middleware in `api.py`.
- The audit that prompted it: 47 write routes, 16 role checks. Sprinkling `require_writer` into the other 31 is the same mistake `v_acct` already cost a round to fix — a guard you have to remember is a guard that gets forgotten, and the forgetting is silent.
- So the middleware decides all three things in one place: whether the request gets in (`guard`), what the database will show it (`scope.viewer`), and whether the role may do it (`permission`). The per-route `require_writer`/`require_admin` calls were removed rather than left as defence in depth — two rules that can drift apart are worse than one.
- `ADMIN_PATHS` (share links, linking a bank, the item list) are owner-only to **read as well as write**: a share link's range says what has already gone outside the household. `ADMIN_WRITE_PATHS` (people, home currency) are readable by anyone signed in. `/api/people/me` is the self-service hole in that, so a member can change their own name and password without being an owner.
- Role lookup only happens on writes and admin paths, so an ordinary page load pays nothing. It is a sync DB call inside an async middleware, so it goes through `run_in_threadpool`.
- The roster is readable by everyone — you cannot share an account with somebody the app refuses to name — but `last_seen_at`, `can_sign_in` and the per-person account count are owner-only. Those answer "what is my partner doing", which is not a question this app exists to help with.

**Login rate limiting (2026-09-17).** `auth.Attempts`. scrypt makes a guess expensive, but expensive on a LAN is still thousands a day against a password picked during an install script, and there is no WAF in front of a self-hosted app. Ten failures per source address in 15 minutes, in memory — only the API process serves the login form, and a restart clearing the counter costs an attacker more than waiting does.

**Tests that cross HTTP (2026-09-17).** `tests/test_api.py`.
- Written because the gap shipped a bug. A split rule passed every unit test and returned 500 from the running app: Pydantic hands the route a `Decimal` and `json.dumps` will not serialise one. The unit tests built their own payload and never touched the layer where it broke.
- Covers the auth flow, lockout, every role boundary, the share link from an outsider with no session, and money surviving JSON. `TestClient` runs the real lifespan against the scratch database.

**Surviving a power cut and a deploy (2026-09-17, after the owner connected 3 real banks).**
- Verified rather than assumed: all four containers are `unless-stopped`, docker is enabled at boot, and pgdata is a bind mount on the root LV (466G, 62% used). Postgres does its own WAL crash recovery, so an unclean shutdown costs whatever was in flight and nothing else. A deploy never touches pgdata -- the image is rebuilt, the containers are recreated, the bind mount stays.
- **What the check actually found:** the newest nightly dump was taken at 21:11, before the banks were connected, and contained zero transactions. 508 real transactions existed in exactly one place. Took a dump immediately (75K, 508 rows) and ran `scripts/restore_test.sh` against it -- passes, 10 accounts, 3 items with stored tokens.
- **`db.migrate` now takes a dump before applying any schema change**, and *refuses to migrate* if it cannot. That is the operation that runs unattended on every deploy against the only irreplaceable thing here, so a container that will not start and says why beats an unattended migration with no way back. Skipped when the database has no transactions (a fresh install has nothing to lose) and escapable with `TALLY_SKIP_PREMIGRATION_DUMP=1`. Needs `TALLY_DUMP_DIR`, mounted by both compose files, and `postgresql-client`, now in the image.
- **Do not trust the pgdata rsync.** `stacks/backup` rsyncs all of `hl-data` to the NAS nightly, which for a running Postgres is a torn file-level copy, not a restorable backup. The dumps in the same tree are the real thing.
- **Still open, needs the owner:** `stacks/backup-offsite` has no `.env` and no container -- there is no third copy. Everything lives on the home server and the NAS, in the same building. And `TALLY_FERNET_KEY` is still only in `stacks/tally/.env`; without it all three bank connections have to be made again.
- Tests: `tests/test_migrations.py` (6; two need pg_dump on PATH).

**Themes (2026-09-17).** `web/src/lib/theme.ts`, `/settings`.
- Two independent axes, not a list of named themes: **mode** (system/light/dark) and **accent** (six desaturated choices). Bundling "dark plus orange" into one named theme gives six themes and none of the twelve combinations anybody wanted, and every new accent then needs its own second set of surfaces.
- **The accent deliberately does not touch chart series colours.** Those are picked to stay apart from each other for a colourblind reader; a theme repainting them would quietly break every chart for the people who most need them to work. Said out loud on the settings page, so nobody "fixes" it later.
- **More contrast** is an accessibility control, not a style: the default surfaces sit close together to keep a page of numbers calm, which is the wrong trade on a phone outdoors.
- Stored in localStorage (per browser, per person — it is a fact about a screen, not about the household), applied by an inline script before first paint so a dark preference never flashes light, and broadcast as a `tally:theme` event so ECharts re-reads its tokens.

**One definition of "today" (2026-09-17).** `db.session_timezone()` + the pool's `configure`.
- Found by a test that had passed all day and failed at 00:11 UTC. `_daily_discretionary` filters on Postgres `current_date` while everything else uses Python `date.today()`. With the server in UTC and the household in California those are **different days for seven hours out of twenty-four** — long enough to move a transaction into the wrong month, short enough that nobody notices for weeks.
- The pool now sets the session timezone on every connection, and `TZ`/`TALLY_TIMEZONE` are wired into both compose files.
- **A fixed offset is not a usable fallback.** Postgres accepts `'-07:00'`, `SHOW TimeZone` reports it back, and the behaviour is UTC regardless — a wrong answer wearing the shape of a right one. `session_timezone()` insists on a real IANA name and falls back through `/etc/timezone` and `/etc/localtime`.
- **The near-miss:** the first version of the `configure` callback ran the SET and left the connection in a transaction. psycopg discards any connection its configure callback leaves mid-transaction, so the pool handed out nothing and every request timed out. Caught only because the suite runs against a real pool. `conn.commit()` in `configure` is not optional.
- The test fixture now configures the session the way the pool does, or the suite can only catch this class of bug during the few hours a day the two clocks happen to differ.

**Backups, verified end to end (2026-09-17).** `~/homelab/stacks/backup-offsite`.

**Research that keeps working (2026-09-17).** Migration 019, `tally/research.py`, `/findings`.
- Every other module answers a question the moment it is asked. This one runs after each sync and **accumulates**. That is the whole point: "Netflix went up $3" is worth nothing; "Netflix has gone up three times since 2024, $84 a year more than you signed up for" is worth a phone call, and only a table can say the second one.
- Two rules the findings obey, because a savings list is worthless the moment one entry turns out to be invented: every number is arithmetic on something the bank reported, and every finding states its confidence. `certain` = arithmetic on facts. `likely` = one stated assumption. `worth_checking` = a question Tally cannot answer from the data, **phrased as a question**.
- A model is never asked to produce a finding or a figure. At most it rewords one, and `llm.py` already refuses output containing figures it was not given.
- Rules: price creep (measured from the first price ever seen, not last month), long-running subscriptions (the total-paid number nobody sees), overlapping services, annual card fees that do not earn out, a balance on the expensive card **with the 3-5% transfer fee named** — advice that ignores the fee loses money — and fees that recur.
- Four statuses that mean different things. `dismissed` is never raised again: being told again about something you already decided against is how a useful list becomes noise somebody stops reading. `expired` is kept rather than deleted, because "this used to be a problem" is worth seeing.
- `finding_outcomes` records what acting was actually worth. Without it the app can claim it saves money and never has to prove it.

**The long view (2026-09-17).** `tally/trends.py`, `/trends`.
- The discipline is comparing like with like. **Every comparison is clamped to the same span in both periods** — on 17 September, "last year" means 1 January to 17 September, not the whole of it. An app that reports "spending down 68%" every January has taught its user to ignore it.
- Leap day is clamped rather than raised on; a partial year is labelled, since a half year drawn beside whole ones reads as a collapse in spending; and a swing under $150 is marked immaterial rather than shouted as a 300% rise on $12.
- The annual review deliberately includes `cost_of_carrying` — fees and interest — next to the ordinary numbers. A review that shows where the money went but not what carrying it cost is a highlight reel.

**Getting out of a subscription (2026-09-17).** `bills.cancellation_help()`, `/subscriptions`.
- Lowering and cancelling are different conversations and need different words. The useful thing about cancelling is knowing what they will say before they say it, so the script ships the **retention ladder** — discount, cheaper tier, pause, credit — in the order it gets offered. Decide before the call which you would take; that is the whole advantage.
- Card fees lead with a **product change**, not a closure: closing shortens credit history and removes that limit from the total, raising utilisation on everything else the same day.

**A regular coffee is not a subscription (2026-09-18).** `analytics.HABIT_CATEGORIES`.

A fast food chain survived at 12.71, 12.82, 12.82 -- two coincidentally equal receipts put 67% of the history on one price, clearing a 60% bar **only if you believe three points**. Habit categories now need six charges as well. A real meal kit or coffee plan bills many times, so the bar costs nothing.

**Alerts that earned the right to interrupt (2026-09-18).** Migration 020, `tally/monitor.py`.
- The page had **53 open, 44 urgent, of which seven were real.** Every rule was true. What they lacked was a meaningful baseline and a reason to still matter — and a list that is mostly noise is one people stop opening, which means the $242 of reversible fees sitting in it never gets called about. That is a worse outcome than having no alerts.
- **amount_outlier**: "Shell charged 3.8x the usual" is arithmetic on a merchant with no usual — a tank of fuel against a packet of crisps. A charge now also has to beat the most that merchant has ever charged, by a margin. *Tried a median absolute deviation first and it was wrong*: Anthropic ranges 18–120 with a MAD of 13, so usage-based billing still tripped. The previous maximum is both simpler and the question a person actually asks.
- **velocity**: eight charges is a lot for somebody who averages two and an ordinary Saturday for somebody who averages nine. Measured against that card's own busiest day — **excluding the day being judged**, or a busy day raises its own bar and nothing ever fires (caught by a test, not by reading it).
- **card_testing**: one small charge at a new shop is a coffee. It is a burst, or a small charge followed by a large one on the same card.
- **Per-rule recency**: fraud is a call you make today; a reversible fee is one you can still make in two months.
- **Retirement, in two halves.** An alert a fixed rule no longer raises is expired — `expired`, not `dismissed`, because the app decided and not the person. That alone was not enough: a rule fixed today cannot reach the alerts it raised three months ago, so "a coffee shop $1.00" was still on the page as card testing under wording no longer in the codebase. **Perishable** rules now also retire once they age out; **duplicate charges and reversible fees never do**, because the money is still missing and quietly closing them would hide the two items most likely to be worth something.
- Open alerts re-take their rule's current wording on rescan. `xmax` separates an insert from a refresh, or every copy edit reports a flood of new alerts.
- **Result on real data: 53 open → 16, 44 urgent → 9, and several hundred dollars of genuinely recoverable money now visible instead of buried.**

**The review queue (2026-09-18).** Migration 021, `tally/review.py`, `/review`.
- The gap: everything built so far is somewhere you GO and LOOK AT. Nothing asked anything of you, so three things rotted quietly — categories drifted and nobody corrected them, new merchants stayed as AMZN MKTP US*2K4XY, and the features that need a human (a split, a tag, counting a purchase toward a fund) only happened if somebody went hunting for the transaction.

A charge earns a place only if a person would change something about it. A $4.50 coffee Plaid is sure about is left alone, deliberately.

Confirming one Amazon charge settles the other eleven without clicking them.
- Two things make it finishable: a recent window, and a line that can be drawn under the backlog. Somebody arriving with two years of history is not going to review two thousand charges, and an app that insists is one they close.

- Bulk tagging is a UNION, never a replace — a bulk action must not remove a tag somebody put on one row by hand.

**Refusing to delete history (2026-09-18).** Migration 022, `sync.apply_removals()`.
- Deleting a transaction is the only irreversible thing sync does, and it was unconditional: Plaid's `removed` list went straight into `DELETE FROM transactions`.
- For a charge reversed last week that is correct. For something old it is not: Plaid serves roughly 24 months, and **past that Tally is the only copy**. A removal instruction for a twenty-month-old transaction would destroy history that cannot be fetched again from anywhere, silently, on one line of JSON.
- Removals under 90 days are obeyed; older ones are recorded and refused. Ninety is generous for every legitimate case — a reversal, a correction, a pending row replaced by its posted twin all happen within days.
- **The bias is deliberate and not symmetric.** A transaction wrongly kept is a discrepancy against a statement that somebody can see and fix. A transaction wrongly deleted is gone.
- Every removal is archived first, including the obeyed ones, with the whole row as jsonb — so "where did that go" is a question with an answer and putting it back is possible rather than theoretical.
- A refused removal raises an alert, because the transaction still counts in every total and a statement will not agree with it. Finding that out from a chart looking odd is not good enough.
- `GET /api/removals` shows what sync has been asked to delete and what it did about it.

## 2. Hard invariants

Changing any of these needs an explicit instruction from the owner in the current
session.

1. **Read-only, forever.** Plaid products used: Transactions, Balance,
   Liabilities, Investments. Never Auth, Transfer, Payment Initiation, or
   anything that can move money. Tally recommends. The owner acts at the bank.
2. **Bank data never reaches a cloud model.** All inference goes to `hl-ollama`.
   No Claude API, no OpenAI, no "just this once" for a hard question.
3. **The model never does money math.** Every number shown to the owner comes from
   SQL or Python. The model classifies, explains, and writes queries. If a
   summary contains a dollar figure, that figure was computed, then handed to
   the model to phrase.
4. **Chat queries run as a read-only Postgres role** against views, with a
   statement timeout. The model can never write.
5. **Plaid access tokens are encrypted at rest** (Fernet, key in `.env`, not in
   the DB, not in git). Backups of the DB are useless without the key, by
   design; the key is stored in Vaultwarden.
6. **Detectors are deterministic first.** Fees, fraud rules, and consolidation
   math are rules and SQL. The model may add a second opinion and write the
   explanation. It never silently suppresses a rule hit.
7. **Every alert is explainable.** An alert row stores which rule fired, the
   inputs, and the threshold. "The AI thought it was weird" is not a reason.

## 3. Architecture

```
Plaid ──(poll)──> sync worker ──> Postgres <── api (FastAPI) <── web (React)
                                     │              │
                                     │              └── chat: text-to-SQL
                                     ▼
                               detectors (fees, fraud, subs, consolidation)
                                     │
                          ollama ◄───┤ categorize, explain, summarize
                                     ▼
                              alerts ──> Discord webhook
```

| Service | Tech | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `tally-db` | Postgres 16 | own instance, not shared |
| `tally-api` | Python 3.12, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy, psycopg | serves JSON + Plaid Link token exchange |
| `tally-worker` | same image, APScheduler | sync, detectors, digests |
| `tally-web` | React + Vite + TypeScript, Apache ECharts, TanStack Table/Query | static build served by the api container |

Python on the backend because detectors and forecasting are data work and
llm-trader already set the pattern. ECharts because it does brush-to-zoom,
linked charts, and click events with no plugins.

**No Plaid webhooks.** The home server is not reachable from the internet and should not
be for this. Sync is polling: `/transactions/sync` every 6 hours, plus a
"refresh now" button, plus a nightly `/accounts/balance/get` and
`/liabilities/get`. Consequence: fraud detection lags the bank by hours, not
seconds. See section 8.

## 5. Categorization

Layered, cheapest first, each layer records its source:

1. **User rules** (exact merchant or pattern). Always win.
2. **Plaid `personal_finance_category`** when confidence is `VERY_HIGH`/`HIGH`,
   mapped onto Tally's category tree.
3. **Local model** (`hermes3:8b`, batched, JSON output, few-shot from the owner's
   past corrections) for the rest.
4. **the owner corrects one** -> prompt "apply to all from this merchant?" -> writes a
   rule and back-fills.

## 6. Fees: find them, then stop them

### 6a. Detection

Deterministic, in this order:
- Plaid PFC `BANK_FEES_*` (overdraft, ATM, foreign transaction, insufficient
  funds, late payment, other) and `LOAN_PAYMENTS` interest where itemized.
- Description patterns: `FEE`, `SERVICE CHARGE`, `MAINTENANCE`, `OVERDRAFT`,
  `NSF`, `INTEREST CHARGE`, `LATE`, `FOREIGN TRANS`, `ANNUAL MEMBERSHIP`,
  `CASH ADVANCE`, `RETURNED`, `PAPER STATEMENT`, `WIRE`.
- Interest charged on cards: from Liabilities plus interest-line transactions.
- **Hidden fees:** a charge from a merchant that is paired with a fee line
  (ATM withdrawal + operator surcharge), and foreign-currency purchases on a
  card whose FTF% is not zero (FTF is user-entered per card; Plaid does not
  expose it).

### 6b. Avoidance playbook

Each fee type maps to a playbook entry that renders with the owner's own numbers:

| Fee | What Tally shows | What Tally does going forward |
|---|---|---|
| Overdraft / NSF | which transactions caused it, balance timeline that day | low-balance alert at a threshold before bills clear (uses forecast, section 10) |
| Monthly maintenance | the waiver condition (user-entered min balance / direct deposit) and how close they were | alert when balance is about to drop below it |
| ATM | which ATMs cost money | note fee-free options for their own accounts (a credit union is on CO-OP; verify and record per account) |
| Foreign transaction | which card was used, and which of their cards has 0% FTF | per-card "use this abroad" note |
| Late payment | the due date that was missed | due-date calendar, alert 5 and 2 days before, flag if autopay is not seen |
| Card interest | interest paid this year, APR, what paying statement balance would have saved | pay-statement-balance reminder, avalanche order (section 7) |
| Annual fee | fee vs. value used (user-entered credits/rewards) | renewal-month reminder 45 days out: keep, downgrade, or cancel |
| Cash advance | the transaction and its APR | alert on any cash-advance-coded charge |

Also: **"call and ask" script.** For a first-time or one-off fee, generate a
short, polite script to request a courtesy reversal. The model writes it; the
facts (date, amount, tenure) come from the DB.

Fees page headline: fees paid this month / year, avoidable portion, trend.

## 7. Consolidation

Recommendations, each with a computed annual dollar impact, sorted by impact:

- **Idle accounts:** no activity in 90+ days, small balance. Close or keep
  (credit-age caveat for cards).
- **Cash drag:** cash spread across accounts earning low or zero APY (APY is
  user-entered). "Moving $X from A to B earns ~$Y/yr." Keeps a user-set buffer
  in checking.
- **Too many checking accounts:** accounts that only exist to pass money
  through.
- **Debt order:** avalanche ranking by APR from Liabilities, payoff dates under
  avalanche vs. current behavior, interest saved.
- **Card roles:** which card to use for which category, based on user-entered
  reward rates, plus cards that earn less than their annual fee.
- **Duplicate subscriptions:** same service billed twice or on two cards;
  overlapping streaming services.
- **Subscription creep:** price increases, free trials that converted, charges
  after the last expected date of a cancelled item.

Not financial advice theater: numbers, assumptions shown, the owner decides.

## 8. Fraud and transaction monitoring

Rules engine runs after every sync. Each rule has a severity and tunable
threshold stored in the DB, not hard-coded.

| Rule | Fires when |
|---|---|
| Duplicate charge | same merchant + amount within 72h, not a known recurring |
| Card testing | 1+ charges under $2 from a never-seen merchant, especially several in a day |
| Amount outlier | charge > 3x the merchant's median, or > 3 MAD above category norm |
| New merchant, large | first charge from a merchant above a threshold ($200 default) |
| Location | a card-present charge outside home region with no travel flag set |
| Velocity | N charges on one card in M minutes |
| Recurring changed | known subscription amount up > 10% or new cadence |
| Unexpected recurring | charge from a merchant marked cancelled |
| Refund missing | a return was expected (user-marked) and none arrived in 14 days |
| Balance drop | available balance fell more than X% not explained by known bills |
| Account changes | new account appears on an Item, or an Item needs re-login |

Flow: rule fires -> alert row -> model writes a one-paragraph plain explanation
from the stored inputs -> Discord webhook for severity >= medium -> the owner marks
it `expected`, `dismissed`, or `confirmed_fraud`. `expected` on a merchant
teaches the rule to stop firing for that pattern.

**Honest limit:** Plaid data arrives hours after the charge. Tally is a second
net and a pattern-finder, not a replacement for turning on push alerts in each
bank's app. The setup checklist says so and links each bank's alert settings.

## 9. Dashboard

Global controls, pinned at the top of every page, stored in the URL so any
view is bookmarkable:
- **Date range:** presets (this month, last month, 30/90 days, YTD, last year,
  all) + custom picker + compare to previous period toggle.

  tag, amount min/max, income/expense/transfer, pending, text search.
- **Cross-filtering:** clicking a bar, slice, or point on any chart adds that
  filter. Brushing a time chart sets the date range. Every chart can drill to
  the matching transactions.

Pages:
1. **Overview:** net worth, cash, card debt, this month spend vs. budget, open
   alerts, fees this month, upcoming bills.
2. **Transactions:** fast virtualized table, inline recategorize, bulk edit,
   split, tag, note, export CSV.

   merchants, month-over-month deltas.
4. **Cash flow:** income vs. spend vs. net by period, Sankey (income ->
   categories), 30/60/90 day forecast with bills overlaid.
5. **Net worth:** assets vs. liabilities over time, per-account lines.
6. **Budgets:** per category, pace line ("on track to overspend by $X").
7. **Fees:** section 6.
8. **Subscriptions and bills:** recurring list, calendar, price history.
9. **Alerts:** section 8, triage queue.
10. **Debt and consolidation:** section 7.
11. **Investments:** holdings, allocation, performance (basic; not a priority).
12. **Chat:** section 10.
13. **Settings:** connections (Plaid Link, re-auth), categories, rules,
    per-account metadata (APY, APR, annual fee, FTF, min balance), thresholds.

Dark and light theme. Usable on a phone.
